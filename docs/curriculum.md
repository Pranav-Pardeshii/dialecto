# Curriculum: CBSE Class 10 Biology (draft for human review)

> **STATUS: DRAFT written from general knowledge of the NCERT Class 10 Science syllabus. NOT YET VERIFIED.**
> A human must check every key point and misconception against the NCERT textbook before any of this is served to users. Edit this file directly; the content JSON is generated from it. Mark a concept verified by changing its row's `Reviewed` cell to `yes`.

Three chapters, 20 concepts. Prerequisites define the concept graph (edges point from prerequisite to dependent concept).

## Review checklist (about 30 minutes, owner: Pranav)

1. For each concept: are the **key points** correct, complete enough for a Class 10 answer, and phrased the way NCERT phrases them? (They are the judge's rubric, so a wrong key point means wrong grading.)
2. For each misconception: is it a **real** student misconception, and is the correction right?
3. Are the **prerequisite edges** sensible? No cycles.
4. Add or remove concepts if a chapter section is missing. Keep the total near 20.
5. After the question script runs: review all questions flagged `verified: false`, all hard questions, and a random sample of about 30 others.

## Chapter 1: Life Processes

| # | Concept id | Name | Prerequisites | Key points (rubric) | Misconception(s) | Reviewed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `lp-nutrition` | Modes of nutrition | none | Autotrophs make their own food from CO2 and water using energy; heterotrophs depend on other organisms for food; heterotrophic modes include holozoic, saprophytic and parasitic | `m-plants-soil-food`: plants get their food from the soil → soil provides water and minerals; plants make food by photosynthesis | no |
| 2 | `lp-photosynthesis` | Photosynthesis | `lp-nutrition` | Needs sunlight, chlorophyll, CO2 and water; light energy is converted to chemical energy stored as carbohydrates; water is split and CO2 is reduced to carbohydrates; oxygen is released; excess is stored as starch | `m-o2-from-co2`: the oxygen released comes from CO2 → it comes from the splitting of water | no |
| 3 | `lp-stomata` | Stomata and gas exchange in plants | `lp-photosynthesis` | Stomata are tiny pores on leaves bordered by guard cells; they allow gas exchange (CO2 in, O2 out) and loss of water vapour; guard cells swell when water flows in and the pore opens, and shrink when water leaves and the pore closes | `m-stomata-absorb-water`: stomata absorb water for the plant → roots absorb water; stomata lose water vapour | no |
| 4 | `lp-digestion` | Human digestion | `lp-nutrition` | Digestion begins in the mouth (salivary amylase) and is completed in the small intestine; stomach uses HCl and pepsin for proteins; pancreas supplies enzymes (e.g. trypsin, lipase); bile from the liver emulsifies fats and makes the medium alkaline; villi absorb digested food | `m-bile-enzymes`: bile contains enzymes that digest fat → bile salts only emulsify fats, they are not enzymes. `m-digestion-stomach-only`: all digestion happens in the stomach → it starts in the mouth and finishes in the small intestine | no |
| 5 | `lp-respiration` | Respiration | `lp-nutrition` | Respiration breaks down glucose to release energy, stored as ATP; aerobic respiration (glucose to pyruvate in cytoplasm, then breakdown with oxygen in mitochondria to CO2, water and energy); anaerobic respiration gives ethanol and CO2 in yeast, lactic acid in muscles | `m-resp-is-breathing`: respiration is the same as breathing → breathing is gas exchange; respiration is a chemical process in cells. `m-plants-dont-respire`: plants only photosynthesize and do not respire → plants respire all the time | no |
| 6 | `lp-gas-exchange` | Breathing and gas exchange in humans | `lp-respiration` | Air passes through nostrils, trachea, bronchi and bronchioles to alveoli; alveoli are thin-walled, richly supplied with blood and give a large surface area; haemoglobin carries oxygen; CO2 is mostly carried dissolved in blood plasma; breathing uses the diaphragm and rib muscles | `m-lungs-contract`: the lungs themselves contract to pull air in → the diaphragm and rib muscles change chest volume | no |
| 7 | `lp-circulation` | Heart and double circulation | `lp-gas-exchange` | The human heart has four chambers, separating oxygenated and deoxygenated blood; double circulation: blood passes through the heart twice per body cycle (pulmonary and systemic); right side receives deoxygenated blood and sends it to the lungs, left side sends oxygenated blood to the body | `m-arteries-oxygenated`: arteries always carry oxygenated blood and veins deoxygenated → the pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood and the pulmonary vein carries oxygenated blood | no |
| 8 | `lp-vessels-blood` | Blood and blood vessels | `lp-circulation` | Arteries have thick, elastic walls and carry blood away from the heart under high pressure; veins have thinner walls and valves and carry blood to the heart; capillaries are one cell thick and allow exchange; blood has plasma, RBC (haemoglobin), WBC and platelets (clotting); lymph carries fluid and absorbed fats | `m-veins-thicker`: veins have thicker walls than arteries → arteries have thicker, more elastic walls | no |
| 9 | `lp-plant-transport` | Transport in plants | `lp-stomata` | Xylem carries water and minerals from roots upward; transpiration creates a suction pull that draws water up; phloem carries food (translocation) in both directions and needs energy; root pressure helps at night | `m-phloem-water`: phloem carries water up the plant → xylem carries water; phloem carries food | no |
| 10 | `lp-excretion` | Excretion in humans | `lp-circulation` | Kidneys filter blood in the nephron (glomerulus, then tubule); useful substances such as glucose, amino acids and water are selectively reabsorbed; urea and excess salts leave as urine through ureter, bladder and urethra; dialysis does the kidney's job artificially | `m-no-reabsorption`: everything filtered at the glomerulus leaves as urine → useful substances are selectively reabsorbed | no |

## Chapter 3: Heredity
